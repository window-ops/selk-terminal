The euro crisis in the game

Selk is set in 2097, and many of its texts point back to the euro crisis of 2009 to 2015. This page says where, and why.

The crisis in brief

From 2009, Greece and other states of the euro area could no longer borrow at rates they could pay. In 2010 Greece took its first loan from the Troika: the European Commission, the European Central Bank and the International Monetary Fund. The loans came with conditions: cuts to wages, pensions and public services, and the sale of public assets.

Between 2008 and 2013 the Greek economy shrank by about a quarter, and youth unemployment neared 60 percent. In July 2015, 61 percent of Greek voters rejected the Troika's terms in a referendum, and a third loan program followed within weeks. Outside the euro area, Romania cut public sector wages by a quarter in 2010 to meet the terms of its loan from the IMF and the EU.

Where it appears

  • The loading screen: tips on the Greek loans, the referendum of 2015 and the Romanian wage cuts.
  • The History section: the reforms of 2045, which answer the objections the crisis made concrete, and TROIKA.RUN, a running game in which Greece runs from 2010 to 2015 ahead of the Troika, with one decision for each year.
  • A late puzzle of the game, built on the euro notes and the countries of the crisis. Its answer is on the developer notes page.
  • The change notes of the terminal: TPANEZA, a program that forecasts debt crises on Earth so the construction units know which supplies may stop.

Why the game keeps returning to it

In the game's history, the crisis is one of the reasons why the Federation of Europe was founded. By 2041 Greece was still repaying the loans of 2010 to 2018, on terms set to run until 2070. The Federation was formed that year, in part as a permanent solution to the crisis, with one budget and one debt shared by its member states. Its reforms of 2045 address the objections the crisis made concrete: an unelected executive body, a central bank outside democratic control, fiscal rules that imposed austerity, and treaties that placed market rules beyond the reach of elections.

The crisis also shaped the setting. Traditionally, investment and industry gathered in the richer states of the union while the poorer states took the cuts. CESEA reverses that pattern: it places the work of building on other worlds in Central and Eastern Europe. The political and design context page describes this in terms of core and periphery. On Titan, a debt crisis on Earth can stop launches and part supplies years later, and TPANEZA forecasts such crises for the construction units. TROIKA.RUN, part of the archive's teaching set, presents each year of the Greek crisis as a decision.
