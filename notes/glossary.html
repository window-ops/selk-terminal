Selk glossary

Science and political terms used in the setting.

Astrobiology
The study of life's origins, evolution, distribution, and potential beyond Earth. In our solar system, several worlds may have conditions suitable for life; none has yet been confirmed to host extraterrestrial life. NASA: Life in Our Solar System
Deep biosphere
Microbial life within rock and sediment beneath Earth's surface, including environments far below the seafloor. It shows that life can persist in dark, low-energy settings. Wang and others, review of deep biosphere research (DOI)
Chemosynthesis
Using chemical reactions, rather than sunlight, as an energy source for biological production. The game imagines chemosynthetic microbes adapted to Titan's chemistry.
Habitability and inhabited worlds
A world may have conditions that could support life without actually containing life. Titan's interior remains under study: a 2025 Nature analysis argues that Cassini gravity data do not support a global subsurface ocean, while proposing near-melting ice layers where local liquid pockets may exist. The game's local microbial ecosystem is fictional. 2025 Nature study (DOI) NASA JPL summary
Automated infrastructure
Robots, software, power systems, and supply networks that build or maintain essential facilities with limited direct human supervision. In Selk, automation could reduce dangerous labor, while its rules and ownership determine whose needs it serves.
Megastructure
In the game, the connected research, construction, energy, and extraction systems at Selk. The word describes the installation's reach and interdependence. It can spread over dusty terrain, stay low, or be partly obscured.
Megaproject
A very large, complex project with long timelines, many organizations, and substantial resources at stake. Management research examines how ambition, uncertainty, politics, and reporting shape such projects. Flyvbjerg, "What You Should Know About Megaprojects, and Why" (DOI)
Prefabrication and large-panel housing
Building components are manufactured in advance, then transported and assembled on site. Eastern European postwar housing programs adapted this approach in different national and local ways. Błażejczyk-Majka and others, comparative housing study (DOI)
Panelák, blocuri, Plattenbau
Regional words associated with panelized apartment housing: Czech and Slovak panelák, Romanian blocuri, and German Plattenbau. The game's robots take inspiration from automating the repeated production and assembly of this kind of housing.
World-systems: core, semi-periphery, and periphery
A framework for analyzing a world economy structured by unequal power and production. "Core," "semi-periphery," and "periphery" describe positions within those relations. Wallerstein, World-Systems Analysis (publisher DOI)
Global South
A political and economic term for countries and communities shaped by unequal development and colonial histories. The game imagines scientific capacity extending beyond established centers of power, while keeping Eastern Europe and the Global South's distinct histories in view. UN: South-South cooperation
Dependency theory
A series of arguments that explains persistent underdevelopment through unequal historical relations, rather than as an earlier stage on a universal development path. André Gunder Frank's "development of underdevelopment" is one version. Frank's original Monthly Review issue
Imperialism and extraction
Lenin's account of imperialism focuses on monopoly and finance capital, capital export, and division of the world among powerful interests. In the story, HX Holdings' control of Titan's output and its hidden ecological costs echo extractive relationships. Lenin, "Imperialism as a Special Stage of Capitalism"
Eco-socialism
A political tradition that joins ecological limits to socialist critiques of production and ownership. Selk's imagined future combines planned climate action and social need with rushed policies and institutional failure. Löwy, "The Ecosocialist Alternative"
Turbocapitalism
The game's shorthand for accelerated, profit-first capitalism that treats safeguards and public accountability as obstacles.
