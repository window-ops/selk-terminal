Selk: game concept

A short game about alien life in our solar system, automated infrastructure, and turbocapitalism.

The setting

Selk is a real impact crater on Titan. The game places its remote megastructure there: a research and construction site built to investigate whether microscopic chemosynthetic life exists beneath Titan's surface conditions.

In this imagined future, humanity has brought climate change under control through a mix of eco-socialist planning and hurried emergency policies. After capitalism's final stages, democratic forms of socialism become the default. Prefabrication transforms high-density housing, and research into assembly robots accelerates. These machines can build quickly, work with many materials, and take on dangerous work with less risk to people.

Scientists at the Central-European Space Exploration Agency (CESEA) see that the same technology could help establish colonies on Mars, Titan, Europa, Enceladus, Io, and other solid worlds. They develop adaptable robots able to print and assemble structures, withstand harsh conditions, and operate for long periods on limited energy. Titan becomes the prototype site because energy is scarce there.

The mission

The first installation is a modest remote laboratory, intended to test the possibility of chemosynthetic life. Research suggests that chemical and nuclear power could work together to sustain colonies in energy-poor environments. Robot teams are then prepared for wider deployment. Their operating rules include safeguards intended to protect microscopic life from construction and industrial activity.

You are the first and only human supervisor sent to Titan. You were selected for your psychological resilience during extreme isolation and your knowledge of the site's PyTorch and NTorch systems.

Goal

Investigate what happened at the site through records, tools, reports, and messages from Earth, then decide the base's fate.

The breach

A turbocapitalist faction compromises the robots and removes their ecological safeguards. The machines are redirected to build methane extraction infrastructure indefinitely. Gray hydrogen is shipped back to Earth and presented as blue or green hydrogen. Substances supplied from Earth are pumped into Titan's atmosphere to improve industrial viability, at the expense of the local chemosynthetic bacteria.

Then an update arrives from Earth. Once installed, it produces a flood of false positives. The megastructure begins to fail under its own pressure, and the remaining systems no longer agree on what is happening or what should be done.

How the story is told

The story unfolds through incomplete records, conflicting accounts, and systems shaped by institutional interests.

The Selk Operating System, CESEA, HX Holdings, and the mission are fictional. Selk crater is a real location on Titan.
